09 — Method

The whole rule, in six lines

No discretion, no overrides, no position sizing. The entire strategy is a ranking, a pair of picks and a monthly rebalance. Everything on this page falls out of the six steps below.

  1. Take fifteen indices. Eight MSCI factor indices (USA and Europe, each in momentum, quality, enhanced value and small cap) and seven S&P 500 GICS sector indices. Two separate baskets.
  2. Rank on eight-month price momentum. At each month-end, score every index by its price change over the trailing eight months. Price only, in local currency, on the index rather than the fund.
  3. Hold the top of each basket. One factor index, one sector index. Nothing else is held, and nothing is shorted.
  4. Weight them 50/50, and reset only when you trade. Equal money in each leg at the moment a leg changes, then left to drift until the next change. No tilt for conviction, momentum strength or volatility.
  5. Trade at the start of the month. The signal is fixed at the month-end close; the trade goes on at the first trading day of the following month. If neither leg's winner has changed, nothing trades at all — which is most months.
  6. Go to cash on a deep decline. Both legs move to cash when the S&P 500 sits more than 25% below its level nineteen months earlier, and stay there until it does not. One signal, one market index, applied to both legs together.

Why these two baskets. Factor indices and sector indices break the same equity market along different seams. A factor leg captures style regimes such as value leading growth; a sector leg captures the specific thing that is working, energy in 2022, technology in 2020. Ranking them separately, then holding one of each, produces a book that is diversified by construction without any correlation estimate.

Why eight months. Long enough that a single volatile month cannot flip the ranking, short enough to turn inside a regime change. On this data the strategy changed its factor leg 36 times and its sector leg 35 times in 127 months, so it holds a given pick for about three and a half months on average.

What the returns represent. Signals come from the indices as described. Returns come from the London-listed UCITS lines that track them, in sterling, on a total-return basis, so dividends, fund charges and currency are already inside the number. Dealing costs, bid-offer spread, platform fees and tax are not.

Why this rebalancing rule. Four schemes were tested on this data: reset every month, never reset, reset when the split passes 55/45, and the one used here, reset only when a leg changes. The first three are indistinguishable — they span 0.16pp of annual return and 0.01 of Sharpe over 118 months, which is noise. Never resetting is the only one that is genuinely worse: no more return, a third more drawdown, and one leg is allowed to reach 78% of the book. This rule was chosen because it needs no monitoring, not because it tested best.

Lineage. The fifteen-index universe and the two-basket 50/50 structure follow a publicly documented approach; the momentum lookback, ranking convention, cash rule and London-listed implementation here were derived independently and differ from it.
